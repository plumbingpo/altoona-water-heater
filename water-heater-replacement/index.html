How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement involves more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. Our technicians match the new heater to your hot water demand, check that the gas supply, venting, and panel can handle it, and take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one. Relief valve piping is done right, applicable code items go in, leaks and draft are tested, and the old tank leaves with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are done to code and to last. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
