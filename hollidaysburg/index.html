How Hollidaysburg Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
