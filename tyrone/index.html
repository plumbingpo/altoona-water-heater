Keeping Your Tyrone Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Tyrone

Water heaters in and around Tyrone deal with a familiar set of problems. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Age matters just as much. Many homes around here still have a tank that was installed a decade or more ago, and once a tank is past the ten-year mark, the anode rod has usually been consumed and rust starts working on the steel from the inside. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless unit in hard water should get annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater may need code items it never had: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor.
