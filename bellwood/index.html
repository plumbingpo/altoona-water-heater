How Bellwood Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Bellwood

Long before a tank floods a Bellwood garage, it almost always shows warning signs. Pay attention to rumbling and popping during heating, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
